# Morgan fingerprint metric comparison

This follow-up compares seven fingerprint geometries under the same frozen MiniMol mean and GP conditioning benchmark. The source-group folds, predictor-role development pools, fixed hidden queries, six endpoints, three seeds, and ten evaluation draws are unchanged. Configuration and checkpoint selection use validation data.

Let $a$ and $b$ be the counts of on-bits, $c$ the shared on-bits and $D=2048$ the fingerprint length. Similarity kernels are Tanimoto $c/(a+b-c)$, Dice $2c/(a+b)$, cosine $c/\sqrt{ab}$, Braun–Blanquet $c/\max(a,b)$, Sokal $c/(2a+2b-3c)$, and Pearson correlation of centered binary vectors. Each is a positive-semidefinite normalized similarity. Their squared Hilbert distance is $2(1-S)$. Hamming uses squared Euclidean distance on bits, $(a+b-2c)/D$. On binary fingerprints, Manhattan mismatch count equals squared Euclidean distance, so they are not counted as independent metrics. Duplicate fingerprints can have zero distance even for different molecules.

Validity follows from normalized inner products for cosine/Pearson, positive powers of Tanimoto for Sokal, the integral representation of $1/(a+b)$ for Dice, and the positive kernel $1/\max(a,b)=\min(1/a,1/b)$ multiplied by a dot product for Braun–Blanquet. Zero transformed vectors use an additional orthogonal unit feature. Numerical PSD tests include empty, dense and duplicate fingerprints. Arbitrary indefinite similarity functions are not repaired using test-dependent eigenvalue clipping.

For each geometry, squared distances are divided by a training-only median estimated from at most 32 pairs per training pool. Radius 2 and 3 Morgan bit fingerprints are compared, with chirality and 2048 bits. Gaussian, Matérn-5/2 and rational-quadratic covariance mappings each learn a positive distance scale, amplitude and molecule-level nugget. Rational quadratic additionally learns shape. Covariance mappings act on valid Hilbert distances. Shared reference noise is retained when conditioning on relative measurements.

The 42 configurations each receive 400 screening steps. Two per metric/endpoint/fold are retrained for 2400 steps, and the best is repeated with two additional seeds. This gives 1260 screening, 420 refinement and 420 repeat fits, with 630 final checkpoints. Step zero is eligible. Validation likelihood determines configuration/checkpoint choices. A separate validation-selected-metric comparator chooses one metric per endpoint/fold before test evaluation and uses the same choice for all seeds. All metrics have equal search budgets. Original direct Tanimoto and its earlier remapping remain separate references.

Primary outcomes are fixed-query Spearman correlation and relative-outcome R-squared on pools with at least 15 compounds. Predictive log density, interval coverage and width are assessed before and after validation-only variance rescaling. Smaller pools, endpoint curves and paired source-group confidence intervals are included. No acquisition-policy claims are made, and the finite search identifies the best tested choices rather than a universally best distance.

Metric definitions follow the [RDKit API](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/source/rdkit.DataStructs.cDataStructs.html). Valid covariance constructions follow [Rasmussen and Williams, Chapter 4](https://gaussianprocess.org/gpml/chapters/RW4.pdf). Dice and Sokal are monotone transforms of Tanimoto, so their nearest-neighbor order is the same even though GP covariances can differ.
